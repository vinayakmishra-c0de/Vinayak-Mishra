Methodology

The "Why Behind The Math" Approach

Every rule gets derived before it gets used. Here's a real example of how that plays out in a session.

How I Cleared the (−) × (−) = (+) Confusion in 5 Minutes

Most students are told to just accept that two negatives make a positive. That rule, memorized without meaning, falls apart the moment a harder question changes the pattern.

Instead, we walk the number line together: facing forward is positive, turning around is negative. Multiplying by a negative means "turn around." So multiplying two negatives means turn around, then turn around again — you end up facing forward. The rule isn't memorized anymore. It's seen, and it never gets forgotten.

Step 1 Stand at 0, facing the positive direction.
Step 2 "×(−1)" means turn around 180°.
Step 3 Two negatives = two turns = facing forward again.
Result (−) × (−) = (+), proven, not recited.
